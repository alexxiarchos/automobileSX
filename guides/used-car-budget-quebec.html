Used car budget in Quebec: what a $15,000 limit really buys

Work backward from a firm total so the advertised price, taxes and other costs fit the same plan.

By Automobile SX · Published November 4, 2026

A $15,000 purchase budget is not the same as a $15,000 advertised price. Set aside the applicable taxes, registration and any separately budgeted inspection or immediate ownership costs before choosing a vehicle. For a Dorval buyer with a firm spending limit, the useful question is: "What can I buy and prepare for use without exceeding my total budget?"

Start with two budgets

Your purchase budget covers getting the vehicle into your name and ready for your needs. Your monthly ownership budget covers keeping it on the road.

Write down the following before browsing:

Purchase planningMonthly planning
Vehicle price and applicable taxesLoan payment, if financed
RegistrationInsurance
Independent inspectionFuel or charging
Immediate work or equipment you chooseMaintenance reserve

An inspection fee or insurance quote comes from the provider you select. Do not assume the advertised vehicle price includes either.

Work backward from your limit

For a standard taxable dealer purchase, GST is 5% and QST is 9.975%. QST may use the vehicle's estimated value when it is higher than the selling price. Confirm the applicable calculation before relying on a simple percentage. Revenu Québec explains purchases from a dealer.

Here is an illustrative calculation, not a vehicle offer. Assume both taxes apply to the selling price, with no trade-in:

ItemAmount
Total available$15,000.00
Amount you choose to reserve for other costs$1,000.00
Remaining for vehicle plus taxes$14,000.00
Approximate maximum vehicle price: $14,000 / 1.14975$12,176.56

The $1,000 reserve is a hypothetical planning choice, not an estimate of registration, inspection, insurance or repair costs. Replace it with your actual figures. A higher QST valuation changes this calculation.

Ask for a quote you can compare

Request an itemized written total for the exact vehicle. Ask what is included, what is optional, which amount is used for QST and what remains payable at registration. If you have a trade-in, ask for its value and any outstanding loan balance as separate figures.

For financing, compare the amount borrowed, rate, term and total borrowing cost. A smaller payment alone does not establish a cheaper purchase. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada recommends comparing the complete financing offer.

Make your shortlist useful

Send your maximum purchase budget, intended monthly limit, preferred body style and planned purchase date. State whether your budget includes taxes. This prevents a conversation about a vehicle that was never within your limit.
