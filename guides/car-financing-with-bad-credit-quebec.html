Car financing with bad credit in Quebec

Prepare the application, compare the complete offer and keep the rights Quebec law gives you.

By Automobile SX · Published August 4, 2026 · Updated November 4, 2026

Limited or damaged credit does not produce one standard rate, down payment or decision. Each lender applies its own criteria to the complete application. Treat any estimate as provisional until the lender provides a written offer.

A credit score is not an approval table

Credit scores are one input, not a published lender commitment. This page does not assign score bands, predict approval or quote a rate. Ask which documents are required and let the lender assess the actual file.

Prepare the information a lender may assess

The exact criteria and documents vary. A lender may consider:

  • Income and existing obligations. Be ready to provide the documents the lender requests.
  • Credit history. Review your own report for errors before applying.
  • The requested amount and term. Compare them with your complete monthly ownership budget.
  • The selected vehicle. Eligibility may differ by vehicle and lender.
  • A proposed down payment. Include it only when comparing an actual written offer; no fixed amount guarantees a result.

Compare what the offer actually costs

Compare the cash price, amount financed, credit charges, credit rate, payment schedule, term and total obligation. Quebec's OPC explains the required figures in an instalment-sale contract. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada recommends comparing multiple complete offers when possible.

New to Canada with no credit file

No Canadian credit history is different from a history of missed payments, but there is no single newcomer program or requirement that applies to every lender. Ask which identity, residency, income and credit documents are required for the actual application. Do not rely on a promised down-payment percentage or vehicle-age rule unless the lender puts it in the offer.

Do not let the term hide the cost

A longer term generally lowers each payment while increasing the interest paid over the loan. Compare the total obligation and consider how long you may owe more than the vehicle is worth. A lower payment alone does not make the offer less expensive.

Do not assume a future refinance

Make the decision using the offer you have now. Credit-file changes and future refinancing depend on reporting, payment history, market conditions and a later lender assessment. No dealership can promise when a score will improve or that a cheaper refinance will become available.

Protect yourself

  • Financing cannot be a condition of a road-vehicle sale in Quebec. Read the OPC summary.
  • Ask whether every warranty, insurance or protection product is optional and how it changes the amount financed.
  • Compare the total obligation, not the weekly payment.
  • Get the approved rate in writing and check it against the contract.
  • An instalment-sale contract must state the right to repay early without fees or penalty. Check the required contract content.
