Used car inspection in Dorval: what to arrange before buying

Coordinate the actual vehicle, the garage and the questions before you commit.

By Automobile SX · Published November 4, 2026

Arrange an independent inspection before committing to a used car, even if you have already driven it and read its history report. Choose the garage, agree on the inspection scope and coordinate access to the specific vehicle. Use the written findings to decide what you are comfortable buying; an inspection cannot promise future reliability.

Start with the actual vehicle

Before booking a garage, confirm that the car is still available. Give the dealership the stock number and give the garage the VIN, model, year and kilometres. Tell the garage about any warning light, sound or concern you noticed.

Independent inspections are welcome. Spiro is happy to discuss arrangements with buyers. Contact us in advance with the vehicle, garage and proposed time so access and any transport can be coordinated before you book. See the buying FAQ for the current wording.

Ask the garage what its inspection includes

CAA-Québec recommends an independent pre-purchase inspection because an ordinary viewing or road test can miss problems. Ask for a written report and confirm the price and scope directly with the inspecting shop. Read CAA-Québec's pre-purchase inspection guidance.

Use these questions to prepare the appointment:

  • Will you inspect the underside and explain any corrosion you find?
  • What mechanical and diagnostic checks are included?
  • Will the report distinguish work needed now from items to monitor?
  • Can you provide a written estimate for any recommended work?
  • What cannot be assessed within this inspection?

Do not ask a mechanic simply whether the car is "good." Ask what the observed condition means for your intended use and budget.

Keep the paperwork separate from the mechanical check

Bring any available service invoices and vehicle-history report. These can give the inspector useful context, but neither replaces an examination of the car. A mechanical inspection also does not resolve ownership debts by itself. Documentary checks belong alongside it.

The OPC's dealer-buying guidance recommends a road test and inspection by a mechanic you trust. It states that a dealer must accept your request to test and inspect the vehicle. Read the OPC guidance before buying from a dealer.
